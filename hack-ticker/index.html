Impact: how serious

  1. Minor: one small organisation, under 10,000 records.
  2. Limited: up to 1 million records, local disruption, under $10M.
  3. Significant: 1–10 million records, services down for days, a national chain hit, $10–100M.
  4. Major: 10–100 million records, hospitals, energy, telecom or government disrupted, $100M–1B, state espionage.
  5. Severe: over 100 million records, several countries disrupted, over $1B, or risk to life.

Impact is scored as if the reported facts are true. Each incident says why it got its class.

Status: how sure

Confirmed: the victim or authorities confirmed it. Claimed: only the attackers or researchers say so. Disputed: the victim denies it. Claimed and disputed incidents have an outlined impact block.

Records are not people

Totals count reported records or accounts. One person can appear in many breaches, and records can repeat, so these are not counts of unique people.

Where it comes from

Every four hours: about a dozen security news feeds read by an AI editor that merges headlines into incidents and updates them; confirmed breaches from Have I Been Pwned (CC BY 4.0); crypto hacks from DeFiLlama; and victims posted on ransomware leak sites, from ransomware.live. Incidents before 31 October 2026 come from a one-off review of monthly roundups and are a selection, not a full count.

The full record, including each incident's history, is in CSV and JSON.
