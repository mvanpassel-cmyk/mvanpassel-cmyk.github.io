The fitting-room pocket test

A pocket that holds a phone on the hanger can still spit it out when you sit down. Take your own phone, with its case, and try these four checks.

Drop it in

Slide the phone in upright. It should vanish below the opening, with room to spare.

Sit down

Sit on a chair. If the phone rides up or digs into your hip, the pocket is too shallow.

Climb and bend

Walk, take some stairs, crouch to tie a shoe. Nothing should slide out.

Check the shape

Feel the pocket bag. Many taper at the bottom, so a pocket that looks 15 cm deep may hold much less.

Why are women's pockets so tiny?

In 1954 Christian Dior put it bluntly: men have pockets to keep things in, women have them for decoration.

In 2018, data journalists Jan Diehm and Amber Thomas measured the front and back pockets of 80 pairs of jeans from 20 brands, all with a 32-inch waist so size couldn't explain the gap. Women's front pockets came out 48% shorter and 6.5% narrower than men's.

The usual explanations: a slim silhouette sells, stretch fabric shows every bulge, and pocketless clothes leave you buying handbags. Back pockets turned out roughly equal, so the gap is mostly at the front.

What helps when you shop: patch pockets (sewn on the outside) and cargo pockets usually beat hidden inset ones. Outdoor and workwear brands design pockets to be used. A zip or flap keeps a passport safe.
